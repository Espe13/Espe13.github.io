Amanda Stoffers

PhD student, Kavli Institute for Cosmology & Cavendish Laboratory, University of Cambridge.

I work at the interface of galaxy evolution and statistical methodology. My scientific focus is galaxies in the first billion years: what regulated their ionising output, and how it evolved with star formation and feedback. I study the coupling between star formation, ionising-photon production and escape that sets the galaxy contribution to reionisation, from Lyman-continuum emitters in the local Universe to JWST galaxies in the reionisation epoch.

Methodologically, I build fast and flexible Bayesian inference tools that scale to entire surveys. I wrote and lead CERIDWEN, a GPU-native, end-to-end differentiable SED-fitting framework in JAX that infers stellar masses, star-formation histories, chemical enrichment, dust and redshifts with nested sampling, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo or variational inference. The method paper is now on arXiv, and the documentation is online.

I am a member of JADES, where I lead a value-added catalogue of physical properties for the galaxies with both NIRCam photometry and NIRSpec spectroscopy, fitted jointly with CERIDWEN (explore it in the JADES fit explorer), and two first-author collaboration papers on ionising-photon escape and on star-formation variability. I am a Co-Investigator on LAPIS (JWST GO 10790), a pure-parallel imaging survey targeting galaxies at z > 16.
