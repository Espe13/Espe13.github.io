Amanda Stoffers

PhD student in astrophysics at the Kavli Institute for Cosmology and the Cavendish Laboratory, University of Cambridge, supervised by Sandro Tacchella.

I study galaxies in the first billion years of cosmic history. I want to know how much ionising radiation they produced, how much of it escaped into the intergalactic medium to drive reionisation, and how both depended on their star formation. Most of my work uses JWST imaging and spectroscopy from JADES, compared with Lyman-continuum emitters in the local Universe.

To fit thousands of these galaxies with flexible star-formation histories, I wrote CERIDWEN, an SED-fitting code that runs on GPUs.

Code

CERIDWEN fits stellar population models to galaxy photometry and spectra. It is written in JAX, and the whole forward model is differentiable and runs on the GPU, so stellar mass, star-formation history, chemical enrichment, dust and redshift can be sampled for an entire survey. It works with nested sampling, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo or variational inference. The method paper (Stoffers, Tacchella & Johnson) is on arXiv and submitted to MNRAS; the code is on GitHub.

The Ingredient Explorer below puts the CERIDWEN forward model in the browser, so you can build a galaxy spectrum one ingredient at a time.

Research

Ionising-photon escape. In my LzLCS paper (MNRAS, 2026) I constrained Lyman-continuum escape in low-redshift galaxies with Bayesian SED modelling and symbolic regression. A first-author JADES paper in preparation infers escape fractions for galaxies in the reionisation epoch and what they add to the ionising budget.

Star-formation variability. A second first-author JADES paper in preparation asks how variable star formation shapes the UV luminosity function at z ∼ 3–9.

A JADES catalogue. I lead a JADES value-added catalogue of physical properties for the galaxies that have both NIRCam photometry and NIRSpec spectroscopy. CERIDWEN fits the photometry and spectra jointly, and every fit can be inspected in the JADES fit explorer.

LAPIS. I am a Co-Investigator on LAPIS (JWST GO 10790), a pure-parallel imaging survey targeting galaxies at z > 16.
